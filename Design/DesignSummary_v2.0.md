# A Dog's Life

## Design Bible v2.0

---

# Vision

*A Dog's Life* is a narrative-driven exploration RPG where the player controls a lost pet dog searching for home.

The dog believes he is lost.

The player gradually realises he has been abandoned.

The game explores loyalty, belonging and the human condition through the perspective of a dog.

Through the dog's journey, the player explores whether understanding the world's complexity requires the loss of hope.

---

# Core Pillars

* Story-driven exploration.
* Atmosphere over spectacle.
* Environmental storytelling.
* Meaningful interactions.
* Quiet, memorable moments.

---

# Narrative Philosophy

The emotional journey of *A Dog's Life* is one of growing understanding, not diminishing hope.

The dog does not become wiser by becoming colder.

He does not lose his compassion in order to understand the world.

Complexity does not replace hope.

**Complexity deepens hope.**

Hope is the emotional constant of the story.

It does not erase fear, worry, loneliness, grief or uncertainty. Instead, it exists alongside them.

The dog can experience hope **along** with negative and positive emotions alike. It adds a layer of nuance to the emotion.

Hope is what allows him to continue through these emotions.

### Love and hope

Love and hope are distinct, but inseparable within the dog's worldview.

Love gives meaning to his actions. Hope allows him to continue them.

The dog searches because he loves. He continues because he hopes.

Hope encounters complexity. Understanding grows. Hope remains.

---

# The Dog

The dog is his own character.

The player shapes reactions, not personality.

He is:

* Loving
* Hopeful
* Compassionate
* Emotionally intelligent, but not intellectually human

His goal never changes:

**Find home.**

His optimism is not ignorance. It is the product of a life that has taught him:

* The world is safe.
* Love is unconditional.
* Those he loves return.

His defining qualities remain intact when challenged. They might waver but ultimately his love and hope win out.

---

## The Dog's Core Qualities

### Love

He never stops trying to return home.

Love gives his journey meaning.

### Hope

When doubt appears, he chooses to continue.

Hope does not remove uncertainty.

It allows him to move through it.

### Trust

His past has taught him that the world is fundamentally safe.

Throughout the journey, this trust is refined rather than destroyed.

---

# The Dog's Voice

The dog experiences the world first and reflects on it second.

His internal monologue is a precious tool and should be used sparingly.

It is reserved for moments that cannot be expressed through behaviour alone:

* Memories.
* Doubt.
* Hope.
* Decisions.
* Quiet reflection.

The player's understanding of the dog should primarily come through his actions.

A wag of the tail.

A tilt of the head.

Raised ears.

Hackles along his neck.

A cautious step backwards.

A lingering sniff.

A glance over his shoulder.

Silence is often more powerful than words.

The dog's thoughts are not analytical.

They are instinctive.

He experiences the world through:

**Question → Belief → Action**

Rather than dwelling on uncertainty, he draws upon the simple truths that define him before moving forward.

---

# Perspective

The player always experiences the world through the dog's eyes.

The player gradually understands more than the dog.

The player often recognises truths that the dog does not.

The dog never stops believing his family is waiting for him.

The emotional tension comes from the difference between what the player knows and what the dog believes.

---

# Dreams

Each chapter begins with a dream.

Dreams are truthful memories which never change - only the player's understanding of them changes.

They are seen through the dog's perspective and therefore remain tonally ambiguous.

The dreams are tied to chapters, with each dream revealing another part of the dog's story.

Current dreams:

* Chapter 1 — Walk through the forest.
* Chapter 2 — First day home as a puppy.
* Chapter 3 — Family argument.
* Chapter 4 — Sign going up outside, mother away, father and daughter alone.
* Epilogue — Walk through the forest.

---

# World

Animals communicate naturally.

Humans cannot understand animals, but animals understand humans.

Humans are simply another species.

The world does not exist for the player.

Every animal and human has their own life, motivations and story.

The dog is not the centre of the world, he is simply travelling through it.

The world should feel as though it existed before the dog arrived and will continue after he leaves.

---

# Animal Philosophy

Animals are morally complex individuals.

Species influences instinct, not personality.

Examples:

* Dogs → companionship.
* Foxes → caution.
* Crows → curiosity.
* Deer → safety.
* Badgers → protection and responsibility.

Instinct shapes perspective. Character shapes choice.

---

# Themes

* Love
* Hope
* Belonging
* Loneliness
* Responsibility
* Morality and mortality

The game presents situations rather than moral answers.

---

# Tone


Quiet. Melancholic. Peaceful. The world should feel beautiful, lonely and lived in.

---

# Gameplay

Focus on:

* Exploration.
* Conversation.
* Observation.
* Helping others.
* Environmental storytelling.

---

# Structure

1. Forest.
2. Cemetery.
3. Village.
4. Home.
5. Epilogue.

Each chapter begins with a dream and contains at least one memorable emotional moment.

---

# Narrative Truth

The owners abandoned the dog. The player gradually comes to understand this truth.

The dog's own understanding remains uncertain and will be explored through the epilogue.

The truth will not be revealed through exposition. Whether the dog fully understands what happened, or simply feels the weight of it, is left intentionally ambiguous.

---

# Design Test

Every new idea should answer:

* Does it strengthen the dog's perspective?
* Does it fit the tone?
* Does it reinforce the themes?
* Could this moment only happen in *A Dog's Life*?
* Does it deepen understanding without sacrificing hope?
