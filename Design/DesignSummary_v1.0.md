# A Dog's Life

## Design Bible v1.0

---

# Vision

*A Dog's Life* is a narrative-driven exploration RPG where the player controls a lost pet dog searching for home.

The dog believes he is lost.

The player gradually realises he has been abandoned.

The game explores loyalty, belonging and the human condition through the perspective of a dog.

---

# Core Pillars

* Story-driven exploration.
* Atmosphere over spectacle.
* Environmental storytelling.
* Meaningful interactions.
* Quiet, memorable moments.

---

# The Dog

* The dog is his own character.
* The player shapes reactions, not personality.
* Loyal, hopeful and compassionate.
* Emotionally intelligent but not intellectually human.
* His goal never changes: **find home**.
* His optimism is not ignorance. It is the product of a life that has always taught him the world is safe, that love is unconditional, and that those he loves always return.

Three pillars to guide him:
* Loyalty — he never stops trying to return home.
* Hope — when doubt appears, he chooses to keep moving.
* Trust — because his past has taught him that the world is fundamentally safe.

---

### The Dog's Voice

The dog experiences the world first.

He reflects on it second.

His internal monologue is a precious tool and should be used sparingly. It is reserved for moments that cannot be expressed through behaviour alone, such as memories, doubt, hope, decisions and quiet reflection.

Instead, the player's understanding of the dog should come primarily through his actions.

A wag of the tail. A tilt of the head. Raised ears. Hackles along his neck. A cautious step backwards. A lingering sniff. A glance over his shoulder. 

Silence is often more powerful than words.


The dog's thoughts are not analytical; they are instinctive.

He encounters the world through Question → Belief → Action.

Rather than dwelling on uncertainty, he draws upon the simple truths that define him before moving forward.

---

# Perspective

The player always experiences the world through the dog's eyes.

The player gradually understands more than the dog.

The dog never stops believing his family is waiting for him.

---

# Dreams

Each chapter begins with a dream.

Dreams are truthful memories which never change, only the player's understanding of them changes. They're seen through the eyes of the dog. Therefore, they can be considered tonally ambiguous. 

The dreams are also tied to chapters - each chapter starts with a dream that tells the story of the dog. 

Current dreams:

* Chapter 1 - Walk through the forest.
* Chapter 2 - First day home as a puppy.
* Chapter 3 - Family argument.
* Chapter 4 - Sign going up outside, mother away, dad and daughter alone
* Epilogue - Walk through the forest.

---

# World

Animals communicate naturally.

Humans cannot understand animals but animals understand humans.

Knowledge is local and animals share myths and superstitions, but no organised religion.

The world does not exist for the player. Every animal and every human has their own life, motivations and story. The dog is not the centre of the world—he is simply travelling through it.

---

# Animal Philosophy

Animals are morally complex individuals.

Species influences instinct, not personality.

Examples:

* Dogs → companionship.
* Foxes → caution.
* Crows → curiosity.
* Deer → safety.

Instinct shapes perspective. Character shapes choice.

---

# Themes

* Loyalty
* Belonging
* Loneliness
* Morality and mortality
* Responsibility
* Hope

The game presents situations rather than moral answers.

---

# Tone

Quiet. Melancholic. Eerie, at times. The world should feel beautiful, lonely and lived in.

---

# Gameplay

Focus on:

* Exploration
* Conversation
* Observation
* Helping others
* Environmental storytelling

---

# Structure

1. Forest
2. Cemetery
3. Village
4. Home
5. Epilogue

Each chapter begins with a dream and contains at least one memorable emotional moment.

---

# Narrative Truth

The owners abandoned the dog. The dog never realises this (epilogue, perhaps? Unspoken? Driven by actions?). The player does.

---

# Design Test

Every new idea should answer:

* Does it strengthen the dog's perspective?
* Does it fit the tone?
* Does it reinforce the themes?
* Could this moment only happen in *A Dog's Life*?
