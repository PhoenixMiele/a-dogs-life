# Act I – Forest

## Layer 5 — Gameplay & Experience

---

## Purpose

Layer 5 defines how the player experiences the Forest through the dog.

The player should not interact with the world as a human would, but through the dog's senses, instincts and behaviours.

The goal is not simply to complete objectives, but to understand the dog, the world and the emotional journey through observation and engagement.

---

# Core Player Interaction

The dog's core actions allow the player to experience the world through him.

Movement:
- Allows exploration.
- Should feel like a dog's movement: graceful, quick and expressive.

Perception:
- The dog understands the world through sight, scent and sound.
- These senses communicate without dialogue.

Interaction:
- The player chooses what the dog engages with.
- Interactions should be natural dog behaviours:
  - Sniffing.
  - Digging.
  - Marking.
  - Observing.
  - Reacting.

The player should never act human through the dog.

---

# Behavioural Expression

The player's primary understanding of the dog comes through behaviour.

The question behind every moment:

> What emotional state is the dog currently in?

Examples:

Excited:
- Swift movement.
- Energy.
- Curiosity.

Curious:
- Nose to the ground.
- Focused investigation.

Wary:
- Hesitation.
- Stepping back.
- Cautious observation.

Alarmed:
- Raised hackles.
- Defensive posture.

Idle:
- Scratching.
- Lying down.
- Resting.

The dog's actions communicate his emotions without requiring explanation.

---

# Understanding Through Engagement

The reward for interacting with the world is understanding.

The player learns about:

- The world.
- The dog's perspective.
- The dog's emotional response.

A flower may create joy.

An old trap may create fear or uncertainty.

A grave may create quiet reflection.

The environment tells a story through the dog's reactions.

---

# Player Behaviour

The game encourages patience and observation.

The dog's journey is not only about finding home.

It is a journey of discovery and understanding.

The player is rewarded for slowing down, exploring and engaging with the world.

---

# Environmental Expression

## Wistful Loneliness

The Forest should communicate wistful loneliness.

It is beautiful, peaceful and alive, yet something important is absent.

The loneliness comes not from emptiness, but from separation.

---

## A World With Memory

The environment should communicate that it existed before the dog arrived.

Traces of previous lives remain:

- Forgotten human objects.
- Natural decay.
- Weathered landscapes.

The world remembers.

---

## A World Beyond Emotion

The environment does not exist solely to reflect the dog's feelings.

Symbolism and pathetic fallacy should only be used intentionally.

The world should feel real first.

---

## A Living World

The environment should feel alive independently of the dog.

Life is shown through small natural details:

- Grass moving.
- Rain falling.
- Animals existing.
- Plants responding to their surroundings.

The world continues without the player.

---

## Storytelling Through Absence

The environment communicates through what is missing.

Absence of:
- Sound.
- Scent.
- Life.
- Familiarity.

The dog may sense something is wrong before understanding why.

---

## Scale and Perspective

The Forest should feel vast, ancient and immeasurable.

The dog is small within a world much larger than himself.

The Forest represents:

**Wistful loneliness.**

Human spaces represent:

**Anxious loneliness.**

They may be larger and more crowded, yet still feel empty.

---

## Change Through Perception

The visual language of the game should evolve with the dog's understanding.

The world does not become darker because the dog suffers.

His perception becomes more complex.

Colour will gradually become more muted as his understanding deepens.

This does not represent loss of hope.

The world has not changed.

The way he sees it has.

The epilogue may return to vibrant Forest colours, showing that beauty was always present.

---

# Sound and Music

Sound communicates the world through the dog's perception.

Ambient sound is a primary storytelling tool.

Music supports emotional expression but should not replace it.

Audio elements should harmonise rather than duplicate.

The dog communicates the immediate emotion.

The environment provides context.

Music evokes emotional meaning beneath the immediate experience.

Silence is as important as sound.

---

# Core Principle

The strongest experiences occur when different systems harmonise.

Behaviour, environment, sound and visuals should each contribute something different.

They should create a shared feeling from different perspectives.
