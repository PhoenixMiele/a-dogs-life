# Act I – Forest

## Layer 2: Player Experience

---

## Purpose

The Forest introduces the player to the world through the dog's perspective.

It is not primarily a chapter of lore or revelation. Instead, it establishes the dog's personality, worldview and relationship with the wider world.

---

## What the Player Learns

The player learns how the dog approaches life:

* He is loving and hopeful.
* He is trusting.
* He believes in the goodness of others.

He interprets the world through the assumption that his family loves him and that returning home is inevitable.

---

## The Dog's Hope

The Forest introduces the idea that hope does not mean the absence of doubt.

The dog encounters uncertainty and conflicting perspectives, but his optimism remains.

His hope is challenged, but exists alongside uncertainty.

---

## The Animal World

The Forest is the first place where the dog interacts with another animal as an equal.

He discovers that animals are not simply creatures within the human world, but individuals with their own thoughts, experiences and perspectives.

The player begins to understand that a wider animal world exists beyond the dog's previous experience.

---

## Player Emotion

The Forest begins in an uncomfortable state.

The dog wakes alone:

* Rain.
* Cold.
* Darkness.
* Isolation.

The environment reinforces his initial emotional experience.

As the dog searches and begins interacting with the world, the Forest gradually changes.

The player discovers:

* Beauty within the wilderness.
* Warmth within unexpected encounters.
* A world beyond what the dog previously knew.

The dog's perception of the Forest changes as he begins to engage with the world beyond his initial uncertainty. 

It begins to mirror his internal journey: his growing understanding that love and hope can exist alongside other emotions, such as fear and anxiety.

The player feels sadness at the dog's initial isolation, then gradually develops empathy and emotional investment in his journey.

---

## What Makes the Forest Unique

The Forest introduces the idea that the world is larger than humanity.

It shows that:

* Humans have not touched every place.
* Wild and natural spaces still exist.
* The dog is small within a much larger world.

It is also the first place where the dog speaks with an animal outside of human ownership.

---

## What the Forest Withholds

The Forest does not introduce the darker truths of the story.

It does not reveal:

* Abandonment.
* Deep loss.
* Grief.
* Significant suffering.

Those themes are reserved for later acts.

The Forest is a chapter of discovery and hope, establishing the dog's perspective on the world and the creatures within it.

---

## Lasting Memory

The defining emotional memory of the Forest is the first connection the dog forms outside his human family.

The player witnesses an initially guarded and suspicious animal soften in response to the dog's nature.

This establishes a key idea:

The dog's trust and kindness can affect those around him.

---

## Core Emotional Movement

**Isolation → Discovery → Connection → Hope**

The Forest establishes the dog's worldview before the story begins to challenge it.

A recurring emotional pattern begins in the Forest: uncertainty creates anxiety, but love and hope allow the dog to continue.
