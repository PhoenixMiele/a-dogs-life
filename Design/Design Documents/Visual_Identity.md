# Visual Identity Principles

## Purpose

To define the visual philosophy of *A Dog's Life* before deciding technical implementation.

The visuals should support the emotional journey of the dog and the themes of the story.

---

# First Impression

The world should immediately evoke **quiet wonder** and invite curiosity.

The player should feel encouraged to explore, observe and discover.

---

# The World

The Forest should feel:

* Wild.
* Ancient.
* Quietly majestic.
* Enduring.

It existed before the dog arrived and will continue after he leaves.

The world should feel lived in, with its own history and identity.

---

# Natural Beauty

The world should remain grounded in reality.

Beauty comes from:

* Nature.
* Lighting.
* Weather.
* Colour.
* Composition.

Wonder comes from experiencing the natural world through the dog's perspective, not from fantasy or magic.

---

# Visual Language

Visual complexity should come from the richness of the world, not the complexity of the rendering.

The goal:

**Simple design. Rich detail.**

The visual identity should prioritise:

* Natural lighting.
* Atmosphere.
* Environmental detail.
* Strong composition.

---

# Exploration

Exploration should be guided by emotion and curiosity.

The player should feel free to wander, but never lost.

The game should be:

* Linear in narrative structure.
* Open within individual environments.

Discovery should come from observation rather than objectives.

---

# Environmental Storytelling

The world should ask questions rather than provide answers.

The player should interpret:

* Objects.
* Locations.
* Animal behaviour.
* Environmental changes.

The game should avoid explaining its mysteries.

Meaning is discovered through observation and understanding.

---

# The Dog

The dog remains the emotional centre of the experience.

The environment should feel vast without making the dog feel insignificant.

The relationship between the dog and the world is the core visual relationship.

---

# Character Expression

The dog communicates through behaviour.

His emotions are expressed through:

* Movement.
* Posture.
* Body language.
* Sound.
* Instinctive reactions.

A dog's body tells the story more than its face.

The player understands the dog by observing what he does.

---

# Current Direction

The visual identity appears to favour:

* Handcrafted environments.
* Curiosity-driven exploration.
* Environmental storytelling.
* Grounded natural beauty.
* Behaviour-driven character expression.

The camera perspective remains undecided.
