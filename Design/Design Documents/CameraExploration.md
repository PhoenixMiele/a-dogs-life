# Camera & Exploration Direction

## Perspective

*A Dog's Life* will be developed as a **2D side-view exploration RPG**.

The perspective is chosen because it best supports:

* Emotional intimacy with the dog.
* Clear behavioural expression.
* Intentional environmental storytelling.
* Atmospheric composition.
* A realistic solo development scope.

---

## Exploration Philosophy

The game will not be an open-world experience.

Instead, it will use:

**Linear narrative progression with dense exploratory environments.**

The player should feel free to explore within each area, while the overall journey remains emotionally guided.

The player should never feel lost.

The dog may be lost, but the player's emotional direction remains clear.

---

## World Design

Side-view does not mean a simple corridor.

The world should feel like a handcrafted diorama:

* Layered environments.
* Vertical spaces.
* Hidden paths.
* Optional discoveries.
* Environmental storytelling.

Exploration is driven by curiosity rather than objectives.

The player explores because they wonder:

> "What is over there?"

---

## Environmental Storytelling

The world should communicate without explanation.

The player discovers meaning through:

* Objects.
* Locations.
* Animal behaviour.
* The dog's reactions.

The environment should ask questions rather than provide answers.

---

## Character Expression

The dog communicates primarily through behaviour.

His emotional state is shown through:

* Movement.
* Posture.
* Tail.
* Ears.
* Hackles.
* Sounds.
* Instinctive reactions.

The player understands the dog by observing him.

---

## Narrative Presentation

The majority of emotional expression happens through gameplay.

Additional visual language may be introduced for significant moments:

* Important conversations.
* Memories.
* Moments of reflection.

These should enhance the dog's expression rather than replace it.

---

## Guiding Principle

*A Dog's Life* is not about exploring a large world.

It is about discovering a meaningful world through the eyes of a dog.

The goal is not scale.

The goal is connection.
